## Goal
Research only (no code). Write the findings into docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md under a new dated heading, then end with a list of concrete future tasks.

Idea (Roee): run a headless KesherAI worker inside a free Kaggle notebook (GPU T4 x2, 32 GB VRAM; or P100) that connects OUT to his KesherAI hub (like his PC does, no inbound tunnel), takes self-work / small coding tasks, runs a local model with llama.cpp (CUDA), pushes the work to a git branch and reports progress to the hub. Extra free capacity next to his PC (RTX 3090).

1) Kaggle's terms of use and acceptable use policy: is a long-running worker that connects out to the user's own server allowed? Are ngrok-style tunnels, background servers, or using notebooks as a compute backend allowed or banned? Any account-ban reports. Quote the exact clauses.
2) Limits today: weekly GPU/TPU hours, session length, idle timeout, internet access in notebooks, disk, whether a session can be started on a schedule or through the Kaggle API (kaggle kernels push / run) without opening the browser, and how secrets (Kaggle Secrets) work.
3) What runs well on 2x T4: llama.cpp CUDA build time, tokens/s for 7B-32B coding models in GGUF Q4, tool calling; startup time (download model each session vs Kaggle datasets/models cache).
4) Alternatives with the same shape: Google Colab free, Lightning AI free studio, Hugging Face ZeroGPU, Modal/other free credits — limits and terms for the same use.
5) Design for KesherAI: a generic "cloud worker" (headless node) that any of these can run; pairing with the hub, what it may receive (only tasks that pass the leak guard, never private code), how it pushes results, how it shows in the hub's agents list, auto-start/stop and quota tracking.
6) Language and weight: what the worker should be written in to start fast and stay light (Node vs a small Go/Rust binary), and what to measure first.

End with a clear verdict (allowed or not, worth it or not), a recommended design and first steps. Cite sources; mark anything uncertain.
