## Research report
# KesherAI Sound Design and Foley Generation Pipeline (Late 2026 Specification)

This research report details the architectural and algorithmic specifications for a localized procedural game sound effect (SFX) and foley generation pipeline within KesherAI, custom-tailored for Roee. The pipeline integrates **Stable Audio Open** and **AudioLDM 2**, handles automated transient processing and looping, and supports direct asset export into Unity and Unreal Engine.

---

## ## Model Evaluation: Stable Audio Open vs. AudioLDM 2 for Short Foley

For short foley generation (<5 seconds), evaluating generation latency, model footprint, and VRAM utilization is critical for local deployment on consumer-grade and workstation hardware:

* **Stable Audio Open / Stable Audio 3 Family:** 
  * *Stable Audio 3* introduces variable-length latent diffusion models that avoid compute and memory wastage by scaling allocated embeddings ($L$) proportionally to requested durations ($d$), rather than padding full-length sequences with silence [1]. 
  * The *small-sfx* variant features 459M parameters (diffusion transformer only) and achieves an inference latency under 0.44 seconds on an H200 GPU, or a few seconds on a MacBook Pro M4 via its optimized open-weight release [1]. 
  * Stable Audio Open uses an autoencoder with a 4096× downsampling factor, balancing compact latent representation with high-fidelity acoustic reconstruction [1].
* **AudioLDM 2 Family:**
  * AudioLDM 2 relies on a multi-stage architecture combining two text encoders (CLAP and Flan-T5), a GPT-2 language model to auto-regressively generate conditioning embedding vectors ($\tilde{\boldsymbol{E}}_{1:N}$), a latent diffusion UNet, a VAE decoder, and a vocoder [2].
  * Standard implementations are notoriously slow (generating a 10-second sample can exceed 30 seconds due to deep multi-stage steps and unoptimized code) [2]. However, applying modern optimizations via the Hugging Face `Diffusers` library—such as half-precision (FP16), FlashAttention, torch compilation, and negative prompting—reduces inference time over tenfold, allowing a 10-second audio sample to be generated in roughly 1 second [2].
  * Official checkpoints include `cvssp/audioldm2` (1.1B parameters, 1150k training hours) and `cvssp/audioldm2-large` (1.5B parameters) [2].

---

## ## Algorithmic Audio Post-Processing

To make raw generated foley game-ready for KesherAI, the pipeline applies a strict post-processing sequence:
1. **Zero-Crossing Transient Trimming:** Scans the audio buffer from the start to locate the first major upward or downward zero-crossing where the signal amplitude exceeds a noise floor threshold ($\epsilon = 10^{-4}$), effectively stripping out leading generative latency or silence.
2. **DC Offset Removal:** Calculates the mean amplitude value across the entire trimmed waveform array and subtracts it from all samples to eliminate unwanted low-frequency thumps and center the waveform around 0.0 amplitude.
3. **Peak Normalization (-1.0 dBFS):** Scans for the absolute peak sample value ($P_{\text{max}}$) in the buffer, computes the required linear gain scalar to map $P_{\text{max}}$ to $-1.0\text{ dBFS}$ ($10^{-1.0 / 20} \approx 0.89125$), and scales the waveform uniformly to maximize headroom without digital clipping.

---

## ## Automated Seamless Loop Detection

For ambient and environmental sound effects, the pipeline automates seamless looping using cross-correlation analysis:
* **Windowed Cross-Correlation:** The algorithm extracts a tail segment of length $L_w$ (e.g., 0.5 seconds) from near the end of the generated buffer and computes normalized cross-correlation against sliding windows across the middle-to-late section of the audio.
* **Optimal Loop Point Selection:** It identifies the lag index yielding the highest cross-correlation coefficient ($\ge 0.85$), ensuring phase alignment and minimal spectral discontinuity.
* **Crossfade Blending:** A linear or equal-power crossfade of length $L_f$ (e.g., 50–100 ms) is applied across the splice boundary to completely eradicate audible clicks or pops when the loop wraps.

---

## ## One-Click Export to Unity and Unreal Engine

The pipeline packages generated assets directly into engine-compatible directory structures with all required metadata:

* **Unity Export (`AudioClip` & `.meta`):**
  * Exports the processed `.wav` file into the Unity project asset tree.
  * Generates a companion `.meta` YAML file containing a unique `guid`, asset import settings (`AudioImporter`), hardware compression choices (e.g., Vorbis/PCM), and `preloadAudioData` flags [25, 26, 27]. At runtime, Unity exposes this directly as an `AudioClip` instance via script [25, 26].
* **Unreal Engine Export (`SoundWave` & `MetaSoundSource`):**
  * Generates a `SoundWave` asset wrapper pointing to the raw audio buffer.
  * Automatically provisions a **MetaSound Source** (`UMetaSoundSource`), which serves as a procedural per-sample DSP graph replacing legacy SoundCues [22, 23, 24]. The MetaSound graph wires up Audio and Trigger nodes natively, allowing Roee to immediately preview, modulate parameters at audio-rate, or embed the generated foley into gameplay blueprints [23, 24].

---

## ## Conclusion

The KesherAI procedural SFX and foley pipeline successfully harnesses the low latency and efficiency of Stable Audio Open (small-sfx) alongside optimized AudioLDM 2 models. By combining automated zero-crossing trimming, DC offset removal, -1.0 dBFS peak normalization, and cross-correlation loop detection, the framework turns raw generative outputs into clean, game-ready assets. Finally, direct one-click serialization to Unity (`AudioClip` + `.meta`) and Unreal Engine (`SoundWave` + `MetaSoundSource`) bridges local AI audio generation seamlessly into Roee's game development workflows.
