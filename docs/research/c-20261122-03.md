# From Sound Editor to DAW Level: Multitrack, Virtual Instruments, Hum-to-MIDI, Mixing, and Local AI

## Summary

This research establishes the complete architectural and algorithmic blueprint for transforming KesherAI's audio editor from a single-track destructive audio trimmer into a modern, web-native Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) comparable to Ableton Live 12, Reaper 7, FL Studio 2024, BandLab, and Audacity 3.6. 

KesherAI operates under a specific design profile: an open-source, local-first desktop application built on Electron, React, and a local Node.js server, communicating with local neural inference runtimes (`llama.cpp`, `sd.cpp`, ComfyUI, and ONNX Runtime). The app must serve two form factors simultaneously: a game developer working on a high-spec PC and the same user accessing the studio remotely from a mobile phone over a constrained 390 px viewport in both Hebrew and English (`L(he, en)`).

To reach DAW tier without introducing audio dropouts, UI freezes, or prohibitive memory usage, KesherAI must adopt four foundational pillars:
1. **Zero-Dropout AudioWorklet & Lock-Free Threading:** Migrating from main-thread buffer manipulation to an isolated `AudioWorklet` processing loop operating on 128-sample quantums (~2.67 ms at 48 kHz). Inter-thread audio streaming from background decoding Web Workers is mediated by lock-free Single-Producer Single-Consumer (SPSC) ring buffers on `SharedArrayBuffer` with WebAssembly time-stretching (`signalsmith-stretch`).
2. **Expressive Hum/Whistle-to-Instrument Pipeline:** Replacing monophonic YIN pitch detection with Spotify's polyphonic Basic Pitch neural model executed inside a Web Worker at 22,050 Hz via `@spotify/basic-pitch-ts` or ONNX Runtime Web. It extracts note onsets, velocities, durations, and continuous 14-bit pitch bend contours ($\pm 2$ semitones) to preserve vibrato, portamento, and acoustic vocal nuances, rendered through a SoundFont 2/3 engine (`spessasynth_lib`) and dual-oscillator subtractive/FM synths.
3. **Studio Mixing Console & DSP Rack:** Implementing a non-destructive multitrack routing graph with channel strips, pre/post aux sends, sub-mix busses, and an Audacity 3.6-style Master Bus. Custom AudioWorklet DSP nodes provide 4-band parametric biquad EQ with real-time FFT spectrum analysis, true sidechain compression with dynamic ducking, convolution reverb, and tempo-synced ping-pong delays.
4. **Local Neural Audio Workflows:** Orchestrating state-of-the-art local AI models: Mel-Band RoFormer (SDR $>9.8$ dB) and HTDemucs v4 for 1-click 4-stem separation; DeepFilterNet 3 for real-time 48 kHz speech enhancement and acoustic room dereverberation (RTF $<0.05$); and Stable Audio Open / ACE-Step 1.5 for generative loops, ambient beds, and Foley sound effects with game-engine seamless loop metadata (`LOOPSTART` / `LOOPLENGTH` and WAV `smpl` chunks).

## 1. State of the Art: DAW Paradigms & Industry Benchmarks

Over the last 12 months, professional desktop and web DAWs have converged on hybrid workflow models combining non-destructive audio editing, scale-aware MIDI generation, integrated stem isolation, and real-time DSP visual feedback.

### 1.1 Ableton Live 12 (Released March 2024 / Updated 2024–2025)
- **Scale-Aware Composition:** Live 12 introduced a unified global tuning and scale system [1]. Every MIDI clip, device (Arpeggiator, Scale effect), and piano roll interface can snap to or highlight musical scale degrees, preventing accidental out-of-key notes [1, 2].
- **MIDI Transformations & Generators:** Adds native panels within the Clip View for algorithmic variation: strumming, ornamenting, velocity curve shaping, and generative arpeggiation [3, 4].
- **Audio-to-MIDI Engines:** Live 12 maintains three dedicated offline extraction algorithms [5]:
  - *Convert Melody to New MIDI Track:* Monophonic pitch tracking optimized for solo voice, flute, or whistle [5].
  - *Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track:* Polyphonic chordal analysis for polyphonic acoustic guitars and pianos [5].
  - *Convert Drums to New MIDI Track:* Transient and spectral centroid classification that maps percussive hits directly onto a Drum Rack containing kick, snare, and hi-hat pads [6, 5].
- **Stacked Detail View:** Displays the MIDI clip editor/automation envelope lanes simultaneously alongside track audio effects, removing view toggling [4, 2].

### 1.2 FL Studio 2024 / 24 (Released June 2024 / 2024–2025)
- **Integrated Neural Stem Separation:** Right-clicking any audio clip in the Playlist allows 1-click 4-stem separation (Vocals, Drums, Bass, Instruments) [7]. FL Studio 24 optimized the separation engine with a dedicated "Safe Mode" for CPU architectures, reducing VRAM footprint and processing latency, with auto-naming and color-coding of playlist lanes [4, 8].
- **Chord Progression Tool (CPT):** A piano roll assistant that algorithmically generates harmonic progressions based on genre, emotional mood, and voice-leading rules [1, 6].
- **Kepler Exo & Vintage Synths:** Dual-oscillator analog synth modeling Juno-6 and Jupiter-8 architectures with integrated chorus and 16-slot modulation matrices [9].
- **Low-Latency Mixer Monitoring:** Dedicated `Delay audio output` track modes to resolve PDC (Plugin Delay Compensation) alignment during recording [4].

### 1.3 Cockos REAPER 7 (Released Late 2023 / Updated throughout 2024–2025)
- **Fixed Item Lanes & Swipe Comping:** Tracks support up to 128 discrete item lanes [11, 4]. Users can record multiple takes and audition or assemble the final master take using non-destructive "swipe comping" with automated crossfades [7, 3].
- **Parallel FX & FX Containers:** Native plugin containers allow arbitrary sub-chains with internal parallel routing and macro parameter controls inside a single track insert slot [9, 10].
- **Ultra-High Channel Counts:** Native support for 128 channels per track, enabling complex sidechain routing, spatial/multichannel panning, and parallel processing matrices without auxiliary bus clutter [11, 9].

### 1.4 BandLab (Web-Native DAW Benchmark)
- **Cross-Platform Parity:** Operating across desktop browsers and mobile devices (iOS/Android), BandLab serves over 100 million creators using standard Web Audio API and Web MIDI API implementations [1, 3].
- **Mix Editor Engine:** Supports up to 16 concurrent audio and MIDI tracks, virtual instruments, pitch correction (AutoPitch), guitar amp simulations, and cloud-synced revision history [1, 4].
- **Mobile Usability Paradigm:** Solves the 390 px mobile challenge by splitting the interface into dedicated modes: a horizontal overview timeline, a full-screen vertical channel strip / mixer drawer, and an on-screen responsive touch piano roll / drum pad [3].

### 1.5 Audacity 3.6 & 3.7 (Released Mid–Late 2024)
- **Master Effects:** Introduces a project-wide master bus processing chain, allowing compressors, limiters, and EQs to process the combined mix non-destructively prior to export [1, 3].
- **Redesigned Dynamics Suite:** Complete overhaul of Compressor and Limiter algorithms, featuring real-time visual Gain Reduction History graphs directly inside the effect window [1, 5].
- **Intel OpenVINO AI Plugins:** Offline deep-learning modules for Demucs v4 2-stem/4-stem separation, Whisper speech transcription into Label Tracks, and full-band noise suppression [1, 2].

## 2. Web Audio Engine Architecture: AudioWorklet & Low-Latency Threading

KesherAI's existing sound editor (`plugins/sound-editor/ui/editor.js`) performs all DSP synchronously on the browser's main JavaScript thread using monolithic `Float32Array` buffers. While sufficient for single-file trimming, this architecture fails in a DAW environment: main-thread garbage collection or React UI re-renders cause severe audio dropouts (crackles and buffer under-runs).

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                              BROWSER MAIN THREAD                            │
│  React Timeline UI ──── Playhead ──── User Input ──── Visual Meters (60Hz) │
└──────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────▲───────┘
       │ Commands / Events (Lock-Free)                                │ State / RMS
       ▼                                                              │
┌───────────────────────────────┐           ┌─────────────────────────┴───────┐
│     BACKGROUND WEB WORKER     │           │       SHARED ARRAY BUFFER       │
│  - Disk Asset Streaming       │──────────►│  - Lock-Free SPSC Ring Buffers  │
│  - MP3/WAV/OGG Demuxing       │   PCM     │  - Atomics Read/Write Pointers  │
│  - Basic Pitch ONNX Inference │  Streams  │  - Zero GC Allocations in Loop  │
└───────────────────────────────┘           └─────────┬───────────────────────┘
                                                      │ 128-sample blocks
                                                      ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       AUDIOWORKLET THREAD (Real-Time)                       │
│  Multitrack Summing ──► Per-Track DSP Rack ──► Bus Aux ──► Master Limiter   │
│  (128 samples per quantum: 2.90ms @ 44.1kHz / 2.67ms @ 48kHz)               │
└─────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┘
                                      ▼
                             DAC / Audio Hardware
```

### 2.1 The AudioWorklet Execution Quantum
The W3C Web Audio API executes custom audio processing inside an `AudioWorkletProcessor` on a high-priority, real-time operating system thread [1, 2]. 
- **Render Quantum:** Audio is processed in fixed blocks of $128$ frames [1, 2].
  $$\Delta t = \frac{128}{f_s} \implies \begin{cases} 2.902\text{ ms} & \text{at } f_s = 44,100\text{ Hz} \\ 2.667\text{ ms} & \text{at } f_s = 48,000\text{ Hz} \end{cases}$$
- **Zero-Allocation Rule:** The `process(inputs, outputs, parameters)` method must execute without heap allocations (`new Float32Array(...)`, object literals, or closures). All scratch buffers, delay lines, and state variables are pre-allocated during processor instantiation.

### 2.2 SharedArrayBuffer & Lock-Free SPSC Ring Buffers
Streaming multi-gigabyte audio files or long multitrack stems cannot be done via standard `postMessage()` due to structured-clone serialization overhead. Instead, communication between background Web Workers and the `AudioWorkletProcessor` uses `SharedArrayBuffer` configured as a Single-Producer Single-Consumer (SPSC) circular FIFO queue [5, 2]:
- **Atomic Pointers:** Read and write indices are coordinated using `Atomics.load()` and `Atomics.store()` on an `Int32Array` without mutex locks [3].
- **Cross-Origin Isolation:** In Electron and modern browsers, `SharedArrayBuffer` requires the Node.js server to serve responses with two security headers:
  ```http
  Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin
  Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp
  ```
- **Library Selection:** Google Chrome Labs' battle-tested `ringbuf.js` (`RingBuffer`) provides lock-free audio exchange between the decoding worker and the AudioWorklet render loop [7, 8].

### 2.3 Non-Destructive Clip Editing & Warping
- **Clip Bounds & Slip Editing:** Each track contains a collection of non-destructive `AudioClip` references:
  ```typescript
  export interface AudioClip {
    id: string;
    bufferId: string;      // Key in AssetStore / Worker Cache
    trackId: string;
    trackStart: number;    // Timeline start in seconds
    clipOffset: number;    // Internal audio start offset in seconds
    duration: number;      // Active duration on timeline in seconds
    gain: number;          // Linear gain scalar (default 1.0)
    pan: number;           // Stereo pan (-1.0 to +1.0)
    fadeIn: number;        // Fade duration in seconds
    fadeOut: number;       // Fade duration in seconds
    isMuted: boolean;
  }
  ```
- **Equal-Power Crossfading:** When two clips overlap or at clip splice boundaries, linear crossfading causes a $3\text{ dB}$ dip in acoustic perceived volume at the center point. KesherAI must enforce equal-power sinusoidal crossfades:
  $$\text{Gain}_{\text{fade-out}}(t) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right), \quad \text{Gain}_{\text{fade-in}}(t) = \sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{2}\right) \quad \text{for } t \in [0, 1]$$
  This guarantees constant acoustic energy: $\cos^2(\theta) + \sin^2(\theta) = 1.0$.
- **Time-Stretching & BPM Warping (Signalsmith Stretch):**
  - Changing song tempo or matching loop BPMs requires independent time-stretching and pitch-shifting.
  - *Engine Comparison:* The Rubber Band Library is GPLv2-licensed, conflicting with KesherAI's permissive Apache-2.0 license [8, 1]. In contrast, **Signalsmith Stretch** is **MIT-licensed**, offers C++ polyphonic pitch/time stretching, and provides a native WebAssembly compilation with ready-to-use Web Audio API / AudioWorklet wrappers [1, 3]. It handles polyphonic audio with sharp transient preservation and negligible phase smear [4].

### 3.1 SoundFont & Multi-Velocity Synthesis (`spessasynth_lib`)
KesherAI currently uses individual static single-velocity MP3 samples from FluidR3. This results in unnatural playback lacking dynamic velocity layers.
- **Engine Selection:** **`spessasynth_lib`** (and `spessasynth_core`) is a modern TypeScript/WASM library purpose-built for the Web Audio API [1, 4].
- **Format Capabilities:** Natively parses SoundFont 2 (`.sf2`), compressed Vorbis SoundFont 3 (`.sf3`), Downloadable Sounds (`.dls`), and tracker modules (`MOD`, `XM`, `S3M`, `IT`) [3, 7].
- **AudioWorklet Architecture:** Features a dedicated `WorkletSynthesizer` that offloads sample interpolation, multi-velocity layer switching, and envelope modulation entirely to the audio rendering thread [1, 8].
- **Generators & Modulators:** Implements full SoundFont 2.04 specifications, including velocity-to-filter cutoff mapping, LFO pitch vibrato, and Yamaha XG/GS chorus and reverb effect send routing [2, 3].

### 3.2 Built-In Subtractive & FM Synthesizer (PolyBLEP)
In addition to sampled SoundFonts, KesherAI requires a zero-asset virtual analog synthesizer for electronic game music and chiptune sound design:
- **Bandlimited Waveform Generation:** Naive mathematical sawtooth or pulse waves produce severe aliasing distortion at higher frequencies. KesherAI must implement **PolyBLEP** (Polynomial Band-Limited Step) algorithms to suppress Nyquist frequency foldback in real time inside the AudioWorklet.
  - *Sawtooth with PolyBLEP residual:*
    $$y(t) = \text{saw}(t) - \text{polyBLEP}(t)$$
    $$\text{polyBLEP}(t) = \begin{cases} 2\left(\frac{t}{\Delta t}\right) - \left(\frac{t}{\Delta t}\right)^2 - 1.0 & \text{for } 0 \le t < \Delta t \\ \left(\frac{t}{\Delta t}\right)^2 + 2\left(\frac{t}{\Delta t}\right) + 1.0 & \text{for } -\Delta t < t < 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
- **Moog 4-Pole Resonant Ladder Filter:** Modeled using the bilinear transform of four cascaded one-pole RC low-pass filter sections with global negative feedback for adjustable resonance ($Q$).
- **Synthesized Drum Machine:** Procedural percussion generation:
  - *Kick Drum:* Rapid exponential pitch drop from $150\text{ Hz} \to 45\text{ Hz}$ over $60\text{ ms}$ driving a pure sine oscillator with soft saturation clipping.
  - *Snare / Clap:* Dual-component synthesis: a tuned dual-sine triangle transient ($180\text{ Hz} + 330\text{ Hz}$) mixed with high-pass filtered white noise shaped by an exponential decay envelope ($150\text{ ms}$).
  - *Hi-Hats:* Six non-harmonic square wave oscillators clustered between $2.5\text{ kHz}$ and $8\text{ kHz}$ passed through a sharp bandpass filter.

### 3.3 Web MIDI API Integration
The W3C Web MIDI API allows immediate zero-configuration connectivity with physical hardware:
- Access requested via `navigator.requestMIDIAccess({ sysex: false })` [3].
- Connects USB MIDI keyboards, drum pad controllers, and digital pianos.
- Listeners capture `midimessage` events (Status byte `0x90` Note On, `0x80` Note Off, `0xE0` Pitch Bend, `0xB0` Control Change) and dispatch them with sub-5 ms input latency to the active virtual instrument track.

### 3.4 Responsive Piano Roll & Scale Assistant
- **Canvas-Rendered Piano Roll:** Hardware-accelerated 60 FPS viewport rendering horizontal pitch lanes ($C_{-1}$ to $G_9$, MIDI $0–127$) and vertical time divisions (bars, beats, 1/16th grid).
- **Scale Snapping (Ableton 12 Style):** The user defines the global root and mode (e.g., D Minor Dorian). Non-scale rows in the piano roll can be highlighted or folded completely out of view, ensuring all painted notes adhere to the key [1, 2].
- **FL Studio-Style Chord Progression Helper:** A modal panel offering common harmonic progressions (e.g., $i - VI - III - VII$ for heroic game themes or $ii - V - I$ for jazz/ambient), generating multi-note chords with natural humanized velocity offsets ($\pm 5\%$) and subtle millisecond strums.

## 4. Hum/Whistle to Instrument: Pitch Tracking to MIDI & Timbre Transfer

A flagship capability of KesherAI is converting acoustic human vocal input (humming, whistling, singing, or beatboxing) into expressive, quantized musical instruments.

```
                              Acoustic Audio Input
                         (Humming / Whistling / Singing)
                                      │
                   ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
                   ▼                                     ▼
      Lightweight Monophonic                 Spotify Basic Pitch
        Autocorrelation (YIN)               (Web Worker, 22.05 kHz)
     - <10ms Ultra-low latency           - Polyphonic voice separation
     - Direct F0 frequency tracking      - Continuous 14-bit pitch bends
     - Clean solo whistle / hum          - Microtonal vibrato & slides
                   │                                     │
                   └──────────────────┬──────────────────┘
                                      │
                                      ▼
                        Key & Scale Conformance Engine
                       (Krumhansl-Schmuckler Profiling)
                                      │
                                      ▼
                           MIDI Event Generation
                       - Quantized Note On / Note Off
                       - 14-bit Pitch Wheel Envelope
                                      │
                                      ▼
                      SpessaSynth / Virtual Instruments
```

### 4.1 Monophonic YIN vs Polyphonic Basic Pitch
KesherAI currently employs a monophonic YIN implementation (`plugins/sound-editor/ui/instrument.js`). While computationally lightweight ($<10\text{ ms}$ processing time), YIN fails on vibrato, pitch slides, harmonic overtones, or polyphonic humming/chords.

#### Spotify Basic Pitch (`@spotify/basic-pitch-ts`)
- **Neural Architecture:** A lightweight (~230 KB ONNX parameter file) convolutional neural network with temporal recurrence trained specifically for audio-to-MIDI transcription [4, 1].
- **Sample Rate Constraint:** Basic Pitch is strictly trained on **22,050 Hz mono audio** [5, 9]. Before passing audio to the model, KesherAI downsamples the timeline buffer from $44.1/48\text{ kHz}$ to $22.05\text{ kHz}$ using an `OfflineAudioContext`.
- **Web Worker Execution:** Tensor evaluation is executed entirely off the main thread inside a Web Worker using `@spotify/basic-pitch-ts` or `onnxruntime-web` [2, 3].
- **Continuous Pitch Bends:** Unlike standard pitch trackers that quantize audio into rigid MIDI semitones, Basic Pitch outputs a continuous `pitchBend` array for each detected note event [8].
  - *General MIDI Specification:* Pitch bends are encoded as 14-bit integers ranging from $-8192$ to $+8191$, mapping to a default pitch bend range of $\pm 2$ semitones [8].
    $$\Delta \text{Semitones} = \frac{\text{PitchBendValue}}{8192} \times 2.0$$
  - Passing this continuous bend array into virtual instruments preserves vocal vibrato, blue notes, vocal slurs, and expressive pitch slides when playing back violin, flute, or electric guitar samples [6, 7].

| Feature / Metric | Monophonic YIN (`instrument.js`) | Spotify Basic Pitch (`@spotify/basic-pitch`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Polyphony** | Strictly Monophonic (single note only) | **Polyphonic** (multiple notes & chords) [6, 7] |
| **Vibrato & Expressiveness** | Stepped semitone jumps (rigid) | **Continuous 14-bit Pitch Bends** ($\pm 2$ semitones) [8] |
| **Input Sample Rate** | Native ($44.1\text{ kHz}$ / $48\text{ kHz}$) | **Strictly 22,050 Hz** (resampling mandatory) [5, 9] |
| **Processing Latency** | $<10\text{ ms}$ on CPU (Real-time capable) | $\sim 200–800\text{ ms}$ for 10s audio in Web Worker [2] |
| **Memory / Footprint** | Pure JS math (~5 KB code) | Neural weights (~230 KB ONNX model) [4] |
| **Best Use Case** | Instant whistle tracking, tuner UI | Studio vocal-to-MIDI, guitar transcription [6, 5] |

### 4.2 Scale Detection & Musical Quantization
1. **Krumhansl-Schmuckler Key Profiling:**
   The algorithm correlates the pitch-class histogram of the transcribed MIDI notes against empirical tonal hierarchies for Major ($[6.35, 2.23, 3.48, \dots]$) and Minor ($[6.33, 2.68, 3.52, \dots]$) keys. It identifies the root and mode automatically with $>92\%$ accuracy on melodic phrases longer than 3 bars.
2. **Snap to Key & Beat Quantization:**
   - *Pitch Quantization:* Notes falling outside the detected key are shifted to the nearest permissible scale degree, preserving user-defined octaves.
   - *Time Quantization:* Note start times and durations can be snapped to musical grid subdivisions (1/4, 1/8, 1/16, triplets) using a configurable strength slider ($0–100\%$).

### 4.3 Beatbox to Drum Kit Mapping
Acoustic percussive vocalizations (beatboxing) are transcribed into drum MIDI events using transient detection and spectral centroid analysis:
- **Onset Detection:** Calculated from the spectral flux (positive spectral difference) across STFT frames.
- **Spectral Energy Classification:**
  - *Kick Drum:* High low-frequency energy concentration ($<150\text{ Hz}$) with high instantaneous RMS amplitude.
  - *Snare Drum:* Mid-frequency noise burst ($1\text{ kHz} - 4\text{ kHz}$) with rapid spectral decay.
  - *Hi-Hat / Shaker:* Dominant high-frequency centroid ($>6\text{ kHz}$) with low overall frame energy.
- Transcribed hits are routed directly to General MIDI Channel 10 (Standard Drum Map: Note 36 Kick, Note 38 Snare, Note 42 Closed Hat).

### 5.1 Mixing Architecture & Pan Laws
- **Channel Strip Topography:** Each track strip provides: Input Trim ($\pm 24\text{ dB}$), Phase Invert ($180^\circ$), Insert Effect Rack (up to 8 slots), Aux Sends 1–4 (Pre/Post fader), Track Mute, Track Solo, Panning Pot, and Track Output Fader.
- **Equal-Power Pan Law ($-3\text{ dB}$ Center):** Panning a monophonic or stereo signal across stereo speakers must obey an equal-power trigonometric curve to avoid an unnatural acoustic volume boost when centered:
  $$\text{Gain}_L = \cos\left(\frac{\pi(p + 1)}{4}\right), \quad \text{Gain}_R = \sin\left(\frac{\pi(p + 1)}{4}\right) \quad \text{for } p \in [-1.0, +1.0]$$

### 5.2 Studio DSP Effect Algorithms

#### 1. Parametric 4-Band Equalizer (Biquad Filter Graph)
Implemented via chained Web Audio `BiquadFilterNode` elements operating on Robert Bristow-Johnson's Audio EQ Cookbook coefficients:
- **Band 1 (Low Shelf):** $20\text{ Hz} - 500\text{ Hz}$, adjustable slope and gain ($\pm 18\text{ dB}$).
- **Band 2 (Parametric Peaking Bell):** $100\text{ Hz} - 4\text{ kHz}$, variable $Q$ ($0.1–10.0$) and gain ($\pm 18\text{ dB}$).
- **Band 3 (Parametric Peaking Bell):** $1\text{ kHz} - 12\text{ kHz}$, variable $Q$ and gain.
- **Band 4 (High Shelf):** $4\text{ kHz} - 20\text{ kHz}$, adjustable slope and gain.
- **Real-Time FFT Visualizer:** An `AnalyserNode` ($2048$ bins) renders the live frequency spectrum directly behind the interactive EQ curve handles.

#### 2. Studio Compressor & Limiter with Sidechain Ducking
The native Web Audio `DynamicsCompressorNode` lacks an external sidechain input [3, 4]. KesherAI implements a dual-input `AudioWorkletProcessor` (`sidechain-compressor`) where Input 0 is the primary audio and Input 1 is the sidechain trigger [2]:
- **Detector Circuit:** Computes instantaneous or RMS signal level:
  $$x_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}(\max(|x[n]|, 10^{-5}))$$
- **Static Compression Characteristic:**
  $$\Delta g_{\text{dB}} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x_{\text{dB}} \le T \\ -(x_{\text{dB}} - T) \left(1 - \frac{1}{R}\right) & \text{if } x_{\text{dB}} > T \end{cases}$$
  where $T$ is Threshold in dB (e.g. $-24\text{ dB}$) and $R$ is Ratio (e.g. $4:1$).
- **Ballistics Filter (Attack & Release):**
  $$g_{\text{target}} = 10^{\Delta g_{\text{dB}} / 20}$$
  $$\alpha = \begin{cases} \exp\left(-\frac{1}{\tau_{\text{attack}} \cdot f_s}\right) & \text{if } g_{\text{target}} < g[n-1] \\ \exp\left(-\frac{1}{\tau_{\text{release}} \cdot f_s}\right) & \text{if } g_{\text{target}} \ge g[n-1] \end{cases}$$
  $$g[n] = g_{\text{target}} + \alpha (g[n-1] - g_{\text{target}})$$
- **Audacity 3.6-Style Gain Reduction Meter:** The AudioWorklet posts the instantaneous attenuation ($g[n]$ in dB) back to the React UI via the SharedArrayBuffer at 60 Hz to render a real-time Gain Reduction History curve [1, 5].

#### 3. Convolution Reverb & Algorithmic Delays
- **Convolution Reverb (`ConvolverNode`):** Evaluates mathematical convolution against real-world stereo Impulse Response (IR) WAV files (scoring halls, acoustic rooms, vintage plates, cathedrals).
- **Tempo-Synced Ping-Pong Delay:** Pre-allocated circular buffer with tempo-quantized delay taps (1/4, 1/8 dotted, 1/16 note divisions based on the project BPM) bouncing alternately between left and right output channels with high-pass feedback filtering.

### 6.1 Neural Stem Separation: Mel-Band RoFormer vs HTDemucs v4
Stem separation allows a game developer to extract isolated dialogue, drums, bass, or Foley from reference tracks, existing videos, or mixed song files.

- **Mel-Band RoFormer (ByteDance / ZFTurbo):**
  - *Architecture:* Spectrogram transformer operating on overlapping sub-bands defined according to the human auditory Mel-scale with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) [6, 2].
  - *Performance:* The definitive state of the art in music source separation, achieving benchmark Signal-to-Distortion Ratios (SDR) exceeding **$9.8–10.2\text{ dB}$** on MUSDB18-HQ [3, 6]. It isolates vocals with virtually zero audible phase distortion, metallic "underwater" chirping, or spectral bleed [3, 1].
  - *Resource Envelope:* Heavy VRAM footprint ($8\text{ GB}+$ recommended); inference takes $\sim 45–90\text{ seconds}$ for a 3-minute song on an NVIDIA RTX 3060/3090 [3, 11].
- **HTDemucs v4 (Meta AI):**
  - *Architecture:* Hybrid time/frequency domain U-Net with cross-domain transformer blocks [1, 4].
  - *Performance:* Strong all-round 4-stem separation (**Vocals, Drums, Bass, Other**) with fast execution [4]. Achieves $\sim 9.0–9.2\text{ dB}$ SDR on MUSDB18-HQ [3, 4].
  - *Resource Envelope:* Highly optimized; runs efficiently on consumer GPUs ($4\text{ GB}$ VRAM) or multithreaded CPU in under 30 seconds [4].
- **KesherAI Integration Strategy:** Deploy HTDemucs v4 as the default fast 4-stem separator (integrated via Python sidecar or `audio-separator`), offering Mel-Band RoFormer as the optional "High-Quality Vocal Extraction" profile when dedicated GPU VRAM is available [3, 9].

### 6.2 Full-Band Speech Cleanup: DeepFilterNet 3
KesherAI's existing `clean.js` uses an offline FFT spectral gating method modeled after Audacity. While fast, spectral gating suffers from "musical noise" (rapidly fluctuating phase/frequency artifacts) and cannot remove room echo.

- **DeepFilterNet (Rikorose / Hendrik Schröter):**
  - *Architecture:* Deep neural filtering framework operating in the complex frequency domain, combining low-complexity convolutional layers with grouped linear recurrent units [2].
  - *Full-Band 48 kHz Native:* Unlike legacy models (such as RNNoise or older speech models limited to 16 kHz), DeepFilterNet processes full-band **48,000 Hz audio**, preserving vocal sibilance, crisp transients, and natural breath [2, 6].
  - *Performance & Efficiency:* Written in **Rust (`libDF`)** with ONNX / PyTorch bindings [2, 4]. Achieves an extraordinarily low Real-Time Factor (**$\text{RTF} < 0.05$** on modern multi-core CPUs), meaning a 60-second voice recording is fully cleaned and dereverberated in under 3 seconds [3].
  - *Capabilities:* Concurrently removes stationary fan noise, non-stationary street traffic, and acoustic room reflections (dereverberation) without clipping the beginnings or endings of spoken phrases [1, 6].

### 6.3 Local Generative Music & SFX (Stable Audio Open & ACE-Step 1.5)
- **Stable Audio Open 1.0 (Stability AI):**
  - *Architecture:* Diffusion Transformer (DiT) operating in the latent space of a fully convolutional 44.1 kHz stereo autoencoder [1].
  - *Output:* Generates high-fidelity variable-length musical phrases, rhythmic percussion beds, instrument solos, and ambient Foley sound effects (up to 47 seconds) from descriptive natural language prompts [1].
  - *Deployment:* Runs natively on local GPUs via ComfyUI (`Comfy-Org/stable-audio-open-1.0.safetensors` placed in `models/checkpoints`) or local diffusers endpoints [1, 3].
- **ACE-Step 1.5 (Open-Source Music Foundation Model):**
  - *Architecture:* Combines an autoregressive language model planner with a DiT backbone to generate multi-instrument arrangements and backing tracks [1, 2].
  - *Hardware Support:* Engineered for local consumer hardware across CUDA, Apple Silicon, AMD ROCm, and Intel configurations [1].
- **Game Audio Pipeline:** When a game developer prompts KesherAI (e.g., *"Dark cavern ambient loop with distant dripping water, 120 BPM, seamless"*), the generated audio is returned directly into a new timeline track with automated peak normalization and beat alignment.

## 7. Game Developer Workflows: Seamless Looping & Interactive Audio

Game developers have specialized audio requirements that standard commercial DAWs frequently neglect. Incorporating native game audio tooling provides KesherAI with a decisive advantage.

### 7.1 Seamless Loop Points & Reverb Tail Folding
When a background music track loops during gameplay, hard-cutting from the end back to the beginning abruptly severs decaying reverb tails and cymbal releases, producing an audible pop or rhythmic discontinuity [5, 2].
- **Reverb Tail Folding (Pre-Loop Blending):**
  KesherAI implements automated tail folding:
  1. The composer marks the active loop range $[T_{\text{loop-start}}, T_{\text{loop-end}}]$.
  2. The synthesizer/reverb tail past $T_{\text{loop-end}}$ ($T_{\text{tail}}$, typically $1–4\text{ seconds}$) is rendered.
  3. The rendered tail buffer is mathematically summed into the audio starting at $T_{\text{loop-start}}$:
     $$\text{Audio}_{\text{loop}}[t] = \text{Audio}[T_{\text{loop-start}} + t] + \text{TailAudio}[t] \quad \text{for } t \in [0, T_{\text{tail}}]$$
  This creates an acoustically seamless loop where ambient room decay continues smoothly into the loop repetition.

### 7.2 Game Engine Metadata Export

| Game Engine / Tool | Loop Metadata Mechanism | Required Format |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Godot Engine 4.x** | Embedded **`smpl` chunk** in WAV files [5]. Godot natively detects loop points from the RIFF header and configures `AudioStreamWAV` loops automatically [5, 6]. | 16/24-bit PCM `.wav` with RIFF `smpl` chunk (Loop Type: Forward, Start/End sample indices). |
| **RPG Maker (MV/MZ/VX)** | **Vorbis Comment tags** embedded in `.ogg` files: **`LOOPSTART`** and **`LOOPLENGTH`** measured in exact PCM sample indices [5, 2]. | Ogg Vorbis (`.ogg`) with Vorbis Comment header: `LOOPSTART=141120`, `LOOPLENGTH=1411200` [2, 4]. |
| **Unity 6 / Unreal Engine 5** | Game audio scripts or native sound cues; typically require split assets (Intro clip + Loop clip) scheduled via `AudioSource.PlayScheduled()` [7, 8]. | Export option: "Split Intro and Loop into two synchronized WAV files" with exact sample-accurate length markers. |

### 8.2 Mobile Layout (390 px Viewport)
Displaying 8 tracks with full mixer controls simultaneously across 390 pixels results in an unusable interface. Following BandLab's proven mobile paradigm:

### 8.3 Hebrew (RTL) vs Audio Playback Direction
- **Strict Convention:** In professional audio software worldwide, **sound waveforms and timeline playheads move strictly Left-to-Right ($L \to R$)** representing the chronological arrow of physical time. Reversing timeline scrolling to RTL confuses musicians and creates severe cognitive friction.
- **KesherAI Solution:**
  - Menus, dialogs, button labels, and transcripts adhere fully to the active locale (`L(he, en)`) with RTL document flow.
  - The timeline canvas, time ruler, playhead scrub, and piano roll remain **LTR ($L \to R$)** across all languages, with time indicators formatted as LTR numerical spans (`<span dir="ltr">0:00.0</span>`).

## Conclusion

KesherAI has a solid foundation in single-track editing, spectral noise reduction, and monophonic YIN pitch tracking. However, evolving into a studio-grade DAW requires replacing main-thread buffer processing with a dedicated **`AudioWorklet` real-time rendering graph** backed by **`SharedArrayBuffer` lock-free ring buffers**. 

By pairing **Spotify's Basic Pitch** (for polyphonic hum-to-MIDI with continuous 14-bit pitch bend expression) with **`spessasynth_lib`** (for multi-velocity SoundFont instruments), **Signalsmith Stretch** (for MIT-licensed polyphonic time warping), and **AudioWorklet sidechain compression**, KesherAI achieves feature parity with modern DAWs like Ableton Live 12 and BandLab. 

Finally, grounding the studio in **local neural models**—HTDemucs / Mel-Band RoFormer for stem separation, DeepFilterNet 3 for 48 kHz voice restoration, and Stable Audio Open for game SFX with seamless loop point metadata—delivers an offline-first creative environment tailored specifically for game developers on PC and mobile.
